# Jack (Zen UX) principles, sourced

Every line comes from a post on instagram.com/zen_ux, read on 2026-10-15. The code in
brackets is the Instagram post code (instagram.com/p/CODE). 147 posts, Apr 2023
to Oct 2026. Jack is a senior UI/UX designer and hiring manager running Zen UX LLC
(zen-ux.co, Dallas). His line: "Why design works on people, in 20 seconds."

## 1. The mindset

- UX is decision design, not screen design. Every screen asks the user to decide
  something. Ask "what decision is this screen helping with?" then remove everything
  else. One primary action per screen. [DSdhcrCjxop, 2025-12-20]
- Design for the mind first, screens after. Figma is the tool you use after you have
  understood the human problem: habits, shortcuts, fears, what makes people continue
  versus quit. [DRIuue2D36B, 2025-11-17]
- Problem framing is the senior skill. Take a messy complaint, a vague requirement or a
  confusing flow and turn it into a clean, specific, solvable problem before opening
  Figma. [DRQCZcuj4ta, 2025-11-19]
- Let the problem pick the research method. Doing a method for the sake of the method
  is a trap. [DQ5jq-HjL5X, 2025-11-11] Solving the right problem is the age-old
  conundrum. [DQ9_7-TD-N8, 2025-11-12]
- Good design is invisible. The brain only flags friction, not flow. Bad design is loud.
  [DTqMvidD7k3, 2026-01-18]
- Balance users through the business, not against it. Perfect UX that does not support
  the business funding it does not matter. [DRNw_7qj-0H, 2025-11-19]
- Design the future state. Ask stakeholders what features are coming so the design
  still holds in two years without a rebuild. Junior designers only design the screens
  in front of them. [DSa9ggLj0Jz, 2025-12-19]
- Users now expect cross-platform, connected, personalised experiences that fit the path
  they are already on (car, house, phone, calendar). One experience that does one thing
  no longer cuts it. [DWScJbBjGu_, 2026-03-25]
- Aesthetic usability effect: the better something looks, the better people believe it
  works. Visual design is not optional, but it does not rescue a broken flow.
  [DQrwKjRj8L9, 2025-11-05; DedCXzpOuBt, 2026-10-13]
- What UX is, in one line: designing apps, websites and software to be easier to use,
  because happy customers make companies more money. [DVCS838D7i_, 2026-02-22]

## 2. Cognitive load and chunking

- Cognitive load is the biggest problem you never see but always feel. Lots of white
  space, chunk information, break multi-step processes up. Good UX feels light and
  airy, not busy and cluttered. [DQNK1jqj4ND, 2025-10-25; DehgzGJt7mJ, 2026-10-15]
- Miller's law: working memory holds about seven items. Chunk related content into
  batches. Information clarity is kindness. [DRBKccyjEB4, 2025-11-14]
- Hick's law: decision time grows with the number and complexity of choices. Need a
  fast response, cut the choices. Complex task, break it into smaller steps.
  [DefL9G0vsGn, 2026-10-14] AI-built apps break this constantly with a pile of primary
  buttons: keep one filled button, outline the rest. [Dd9zrlfTqoY, 2026-10-01]
- White space reduces sensory load. Less input, more comfort. [DR0YyYmD4Xd, 2025-12-04]
- Hide advanced settings. People want the basics up front and the nitty gritty tucked
  away until needed. Less exposure, less overwhelm. [DSRAbCkDEMc, 2025-12-15]
- Clear defaults. Most users will not customise, so design for that. Helpful empty
  states guide instead of confuse. Visible progress indicators tell users where they are.
  [DU6cnEQEkSZ, 2026-02-19]
- Airbnb's filters do not dump every option at once. They chunk amenities, rooms and
  bathrooms, and double as discovery because you do not know what you want until you
  see it. [DRLaE2DDwNA, 2025-11-18]
- Amazon's failure mode: an overwhelming home page with no clear hierarchy, orders
  buried under filters, and product pages that stack similar items, bought-together and
  you-might-like above the actual product details with no summary. [DQDYxOdjGx8,
  2025-10-21]
- LA Fitness's failure mode: no hierarchy on entry, a menu with too many options, the
  membership card buried at the bottom of the nav. Looks bad, so people do not want to
  use it. [DPz8J7njLir, 2025-10-15]

## 3. Hierarchy, Gestalt and layout

- Law of proximity: things near each other read as a group. Equal gaps everywhere make
  grouping impossible. Small gaps inside a group, bigger gaps between groups.
  [DefUpjbPf7_, 2026-10-14]
- Gestalt literacy is the 30-second hiring tell: proximity, alignment, hierarchy,
  grouping, visual rhythm. Without it screens feel off even when nobody can name the
  rule. Users feel bad design instantly. [DSRDTwYDL94, 2025-12-15]
- Perfect edge alignment. Eyes follow edges to understand structure; lined-up edges feel
  psychologically safe. Notion and ChatGPT line everything up. Use autolayout.
  [DSYYarpDzMT, 2025-12-18; DRBDvsmjw_r, 2025-11-14]
- Symmetry equals predictability, and predictability feels safe. Centred logos, even
  spacing, mirrored layouts. [DQ-Cxs0jwu8, 2025-11-13]
- Von Restorff effect: the brain notices the one thing that stands out. One standout
  element per screen equals clearer decisions. [DR0Y-kDD1Jq, 2025-12-04]
- F-pattern: top left, across, then down. Place primary and secondary buttons where the
  scan path lands and you know what users will tap before they do. [DSGb0bmD57c,
  2025-12-11]
- Choice architecture: whatever option is highlighted gets chosen. The design decides
  before the user does. [DSGaVMsj63f, 2025-12-11]
- Large titles are visual anchors. A big title gives the eye a starting point and tells
  you where you are; a tiny header feels cluttered and lost. [DSD0sI8j7lD, 2025-12-10]
- Sticky headers keep context visible: you always know where you are and how to get
  out. Cognitive support, not convenience. [DSDuFmzD4Ke, 2025-12-10]
- Stop centring everything. Centred works for a headline, supporting line and button.
  It fails for paragraphs, forms and settings. Never centre four lines of copy because
  it "looks clean". [DehjAIjj3hT, 2026-10-15]
- Use a spacing system: 8, 16, 24, 32, 48. Stop using 13px, 27px, 31px. When in doubt,
  add more space. Beginner UIs need breathing room, not decoration. [DehjAIjj3hT]
- Button hierarchy: primary is the thing you want them to do, secondary is another
  option, tertiary is low priority. If four buttons scream, none carries weight. If every
  button looks the same, none has emphasis. [DehjAIjj3hT; DeaG_ucv3rh, 2026-10-12]
- Thumb reach. Primary actions belong at the bottom of a phone screen; the brain picks
  the lowest-effort action. [DSQ9-qZD4Im, 2025-12-15]
- Readability first: text too small, missing contextual actions (search not on the shop
  tab), unclear identity (fashion app or shopping app), weak hierarchy. [DQZ3n6RD5Om,
  2025-10-29] Readability, tactile feel on mobile and text formatting clean up a UI
  fast. [DQWzwvmEQz5, 2025-10-28]
- Dashboards: an uneven amount of clustered information is often ideal. [CsARna1Odlw,
  2023-05-09]

## 4. Colour, shape, depth, motion

- Colour is direction, not decoration. Neutral backgrounds, soft cards, muted surfaces,
  light typography, so the one important thing stands out. When everything is colourful
  nothing feels important. [DRe5-bskcw_, 2025-11-25]
- Luxury is restraint. Beige, off-white and charcoal signal maturity and trust; bright
  colours read loud and attention-seeking and overstimulate the brain. [DQ-A9zuD2v1,
  2025-11-13; DR3Xr1TjAw0, 2025-12-05]
- Rounded corners feel safe, friendly and human; sharp corners trigger alertness
  (evolutionary: points mean danger). Square reads strict and serious. Neither is wrong,
  pick by the visual language you want. [DQpewXsjy5D, 2025-11-05; DeSIeSKviIg,
  2026-10-09]
- Soft shadows mimic the real world. Depth shows hierarchy and what is clickable, and
  depth reduces uncertainty. Hard shadows pull focus to the shadow instead of the
  element. [DQ2uTgaDx1Y, 2025-11-10; DRvOKqoD2jz, 2025-12-02]
- Soft motion reassures the brain that things are working; a predictable rhythm is
  calming. Motion is emotional design. Relaxing apps blend UI finesse with animation
  smoothness. [DRvQQEAjxAf, 2025-12-02; DQxbB7Ej9zD, 2025-11-08]
- Semantic colour: red means danger or stop and hijacks focus; green and yellow mean go
  or middle state. Red badges dominate attention, so use them on purpose.
  [DSV3zxdj7W6, 2025-12-17; DSI3f6kjyLo, 2025-12-12]
- Dark mode looks sleek and cuts glare but low contrast makes text harder to process
  and scan. Light mode reads better. Handle dark mode carefully. [DSDt9D8D_zO,
  2025-12-10]
- Liquid glass (iOS 26): soft layers, low opacity and real-looking glass read as realism,
  but visibility and accessibility suffer. [DQsBLA9D_GP, 2025-11-06]
- Contrast that breaks the pattern interrupts the brain. Instagram's story ring is one
  colour shift against a flat feed and it pulls billions into stories. [DRPp9V3DyVY,
  2025-11-19]

## 5. Feedback, trust, consistency

- Trust is built with feelings, not features. Predictable products let users relax:
  buttons behave the same way, text sounds human, feedback lands right after the
  action. Design for trust, not trends. [DQNIo7hj1jp, 2025-10-25; DRftKjzDwjd,
  2025-11-26]
- Inconsistency reads as uncertainty, and uncertainty feels unsafe. Spacing that
  changes between screens, icons that do not match tone, one part of the product feeling
  different from another. Consistency is invisible until it is broken. [DRQEaL6D0ex,
  2025-11-20]
- Familiar layouts (nav at the bottom, search at the top) lower cognitive load because
  nobody has to relearn anything. Predictability builds trust. [DQudzpwjzdZ, 2025-11-06]
- Good UI does not teach new actions; it reuses actions the brain already knows.
  Switches, dials and buttons feel natural because they copy the physical world.
  [DRe9XfSEQ9o, 2025-11-25]
- Progress bars turn waiting into progress and cut anxiety. Skeleton loaders give
  instant feedback; a tap with no visible response makes users feel lost or wrong.
  [DQpa1Atj8k1, 2025-11-05]
- Haptics are a tiny "yes, that worked", and work for errors too. Micro delight (the iOS
  toggle snap) triggers small dopamine rewards that add up. Toggles satisfy because they
  give snappy feedback and a feeling of control. [DSDwekyj4z6, 2025-12-10; DSLitGtD7pd,
  2025-12-13; DSV9V8OjxJD, 2025-12-17; DQxc2wTD4L0, 2025-11-08]
- Endowed progress effect: people finish what feels started. Apps show 20% complete
  after onboarding before you have done anything. [DeaMLbDvBkc, 2026-10-12]
- Zeigarnik effect and rewards: Starbucks shows partially completed goals (drinks left
  to earn stars), greets you by name, remembers orders, and uses seasonal UI to make
  coffee an experience. [DQXIo_SD9Kf, 2025-10-28]
- Duolingo: sounds, animation and visual reinforcement keep dopamine high; clear
  progress and rewards make learning fun; education design is visible in every screen.
  [DQXdQ8FjxbS, 2025-10-29]
- Notifications work like slot machines: variable reward plus a red dot. Engineered
  dopamine, so use it deliberately. [DSI3f6kjyLo, 2025-12-12]
- Icons are only universal when the meaning is universal; otherwise they are tiny
  puzzles. Symbol ambiguity stalls the brain. Clear labels reduce friction. Clarity
  beats cleverness. [DRe3RUIAFzN, 2025-11-25]
- Chick-fil-A: high customisation, clean step-by-step flow, rewards built into
  checkout, menu separated out and easy to scroll. [DQIHYcvD7e7, 2025-10-23]
- Onboarding that works is playful and teaches by doing. [DTyn4W7D_dy, 2026-01-22]

## 6. Copy and microcopy

- Write like a human. "You're all set." not "Submission successfully processed." "That
  password doesn't match." not "Authentication failed." Good UX is how clearly it
  communicates. [DehjAIjj3hT, 2026-10-15]
- Microcopy is emotional guidance. Friendly, human text tells users what is happening
  and what to do next. Cold robotic text creates uncertainty, and uncertainty feels
  unsafe. [DSBVRTajx44, 2025-12-09]
- Replace vague labels like "Submit" with outcome-based ones. Error messages explain
  what happened and what to do next. Users should always know what happens after a
  click. [DTd-EBsj1Zh, 2026-01-14]
- Search a landing page for "seamlessly", "effortlessly", "powerful", "all-in-one".
  Each is a sentence AI wrote because it does not know what the product does. Swap it
  for what users actually do on the platform. [DeP92BrvAhJ, 2026-10-08]

## 7. The AI-generated look (his 2026 series)

- Rules he gives an AI builder before "Build": no purple, no emoji icons, one filled
  button, real spacing. AI-built apps look the same because nobody talks to the AI
  first. [DdzTd8CvgTy, 2026-09-27]
- Pretty UI is not production UI. Production-ready means tokens and variables for colour
  and radius, documented components for web and mobile, developer-friendly structure.
  [DWY7K-ogN05, 2026-03-27; DWB201dAN6z, 2026-03-18] (both are ads for the Silk system)
- A landing page he rates: visual-first, demo-heavy so you see the product, builds
  credibility quickly, one call to action, something interactive that rewards the mouse.
  [DQco6kyD0oM, 2025-10-31; DQcrSH_j75h, 2025-10-31; DQXfjTyj7sU, 2025-10-29]
- The 3-second test: show a screen for three seconds. If the purpose or the call to
  action is not obvious, it is a hierarchy or emphasis problem. [DQS1H8mDEeH,
  2025-10-27]

## 8. Validation and research

- Pick one real product, find one moment of confusion, explain what is wrong and why,
  propose one realistic improvement, build and test it with people around you. That is
  UX thinking, and a portfolio project. [DTL5y-NDyt6, 2026-01-07]
- Synthesis is the skill nobody teaches: turning messy research into clear insights and
  action items. Where methods agree, where they contradict, what surprised you. The
  tension between methods is the story. [DSBOA7_D6pB, 2025-12-09; DU1f9Dpj3rr,
  2026-02-17]
- Eight-week playbook: fundamentals before tools (mental models, cognitive load, Hick's
  law, feedback loops); then research and problem statements; then small fixes to one
  flow; then UX writing. [DTd-EBsj1Zh, 2026-01-14]
- AI in the workflow: meeting transcripts (Tactiq), ChatGPT deep research when you
  have no users, NotebookLM podcasts for digesting papers, an agent to scour the web,
  comparative and analogous research front-loaded. [DT5n0xDEcxc, 2026-01-24]
- Dribbble is a visual playground, not a source of UX craft. Replicate real design
  systems instead. [DVPRwa7D2yn, 2026-02-27; DTgeeNOj9Yy, 2026-01-15]

## 9. Portfolios, interviews, hiring (he is the hiring manager)

- A portfolio is a window into your brain, not a gallery. Show the why, the user
  behaviour, the emotional pain, how data changed direction. [DRPMiRHEUa_, 2025-11-19]
- Structure: success metrics up top, no big text blocks, say why each research method
  was run, show how methods connect, add what you would improve next. Order projects
  with the one you want seen first. Scannable beats perfect. [DTBSbpIDyZg, 2026-01-02;
  DS3Ik-9D2sm, 2025-12-30; DS-6iWIkzQz, 2026-01-02]
- The interview killer: not being able to explain a decision. "Why did you do that?"
  needs an answer about the user's mindset at that moment. Clarity is the hireable
  skill. [DS3KmyQD6RR, 2025-12-30; DRPx7q1D6Va, 2025-11-19; DSBN4dHj2eB, 2025-12-09]
- Case studies do not need to be real industry work. Solve a problem for someone near
  you and show the impact and the thinking. Tailor the portfolio to the role: visual
  roles need polish, UX roles need research synthesis. [DUB5Hh6Dz9I, 2026-01-28;
  DS3Dx8cD0s4, 2025-12-30]
- Careers come from conversations, not applications. Two or three thoughtful messages a
  week. A mentor is someone just far enough ahead to pull you forward. [DSx2dfvD7vZ,
  2025-12-27; DRfqmZPD26A, 2025-11-26; DS_BoPaEjA6, 2026-01-02]
- His book list: Universal Methods of Design, Universal Methods of Ethical Design, The
  Creative Act, Don't Make Me Think Revisited, Design Think Make Break Repeat, Designing
  with the Mind in Mind, The Design of Everyday Things, Technology as Experience, This
  Human, Type on Screen. [DQhuBhhjxgV, 2025-11-02]
